Pochettino's Identity Project, Graded

How USA actually played.

Vertical. Intense. Possession-with-purpose. That was the read in May, and it mostly held. This page stays exactly as I wrote it before the opener, because the rare preview that can face its own grades should get to. Under every section there is now a grade: what we called, what Pochettino actually did, and where the tournament answered back. Three wins, a group title, a ten-man knockout win, and a Round of 16 ending. The homework is below.

The shape

4-2-3-1, with a 3-4-3 in the pocket.

Default: 4-2-3-1 (speculation)
The base formation

One striker (Balogun). Three behind: Pulisic on the left, Reyna or Tillman in the middle, Weah on the right. Two pivot midfielders: Adams paired with McKennie or Berhalter. Back four: Robinson, Ream, Richards, Scally (or Dest if healthy).

Why this shape: protects Pulisic's freedom, gives Adams cover, lets the fullbacks push high when the build-up is settled.

Note on accuracy: the specific starting eleven is speculation. Pochettino has not announced it. Adams as the 6 and Pulisic on the left are the only structural locks. Everything else could shift between the Senegal and Germany friendlies before the opener.

Grade: the shape call missed, and I respect why. Pochettino opened the Australia match in a 3-5-2 this page never saw coming: Freese; Richards, Ream, Robinson; Dest, McKennie, Adams, Tillman, Freeman; Pepi and Balogun up top. Then he rotated the side wholesale for Türkiye, Turner in goal, Reyna and Berhalter starting, and still won the group. My "structural locks" were half right. Adams held the middle, but Pulisic's calf took the left wing off the board by match two. The lesson: I predicted a formation, and he brought a whole wardrobe.

Plan B: 3-4-3
Game-state alternate

Three centerbacks (Ream, Richards, McKenzie). Wing-backs (Robinson, Weah) wider. Three forwards (Pulisic, Balogun, Reyna).

When Pochettino reaches for it: against a packed midfield like Paraguay's. Or when chasing late.

Grade: I sketched a back three as the break-glass alternate, and he made a back three the opening act in Seattle. Wrong card, right instinct. The names were off too, Dest and Freeman as the wide men instead of my guesses. Beyond the verified lineups I will not pretend to know his chalkboard. The honest summary is that the flexibility this card predicted was real, just pointed somewhere I did not call.

The spine

The three relationships that decide everything.

Adams + (McKennie or Berhalter)
The double pivot

With Yunus Musah controversially left off the final 26, the double pivot will pair Tyler Adams with either Weston McKennie (physicality, late runs) or Sebastian Berhalter (passing range, MLS form). Adams' health is the foundation of everything. The partner choice is Pochettino's biggest tactical question.

Grade: the foundation held. Adams' health, the thing this whole page was quietly nervous about, lasted the full run. And the partner question got a bigger answer than either name: against Australia he lined up with McKennie and Tillman both on the field in a midfield five, and Berhalter got his start and his goal in the Türkiye rotation. Pochettino did not choose a partner. He used all of them.

Pulisic + Balogun
The decisive connection

The 10-and-9 axis. Pulisic drifting in from the left, Balogun playing on the shoulder. Goals at this tournament will mostly come through this pair.

Grade: half right, and the wrong half scored. Balogun delivered three goals, the opener brace and the first goal of the Bosnia knockout win. Pulisic scored zero all summer, and he still ran the Paraguay opener, forcing the own goal and setting up Balogun, before the calf took him at halftime and the Belgium boot ended his tournament in Seattle. The axis was real, it just flowed one direction: Christian creating, Folarin finishing. But "goals will mostly come through this pair" undersold everyone else, because the goals came from all over the roster. More on that below, gladly.

Robinson + Ream
The left side back

Jedi gives the team width going forward. Ream holds shape behind him. Without that balance, USA is leaking transitions on the left.

Grade: both showed up, sometimes as parts of a back three instead of the left side of a four. Ream wore the armband at 38 and gave the tournament every ounce of dignity we hoped for. And the bolded warning aged painfully well, just not as a left-side problem: when Belgium came in transition, that is where the tournament ended. I wrote "leaking transitions" in May and watched it happen in July.

Where it could break

Honest assessment.

Pulisic's form

Seventeen league matches without a goal, a drought running since December 28. He comes in cold. If the opener goes poorly, the team's psychological center of gravity is in trouble.

Grade: it broke, but differently than feared. The drought never ended. Zero goals all summer. And the fear underneath this ledger was still wrong, because the opener did not go poorly, it went 4-1, and Christian was the reason: the forced own goal, the assist on Balogun's first, a half of pure conduction before the calf pulled him. Then he missed Australia, and the Tielemans boot in Seattle finished it. He never scored and he still mattered. I did not have that outcome on my card.

Backline vs elite attacks

Belgium beat USA 5-2 in March. Portugal beat them 2-0 three days later. The Pochettino back four can be unsettled by combinations and pace. Against Brazil, Spain, or Türkiye, this is the question.

Grade: exactly as feared. Belgium 4-1 in Seattle. The March 5-2 was not a bad night, it was an omen, and the same opponent delivered the confirmation in the Round of 16. For four matches the backline was good enough, two clean sheets included. Then the one elite attack on our path asked the question at full volume and got the same answer as March. This is the ledger line that graded itself, and I take no joy in the A.

Set-piece defense

USA has been good attacking from set pieces (Pulisic delivery is elite). They have been less good defending them. Türkiye's Çalhanoğlu is a perfect storm of a corner taker.

Grade: mixed, and the good half was gorgeous. The feared dead-ball collapse never came. Belgium's goals arrived in open play and transition, not off restarts. Meanwhile the set-piece attack this ledger mentioned in passing carried us all tournament: Trusty off a corner three minutes into the Türkiye match, then Tillman free kicks in both knockout games. We spent May worrying about defending dead balls and spent the summer scoring from them.

Sub depth in attack

If Pulisic or Balogun cools off, the dropoff is real. Aaronson, Tillman, and Wright are useful pieces, but none is the same level of solo creator.

Grade: wrong in the best possible way. The dropoff we feared turned out to be the engine. Trusty, Berhalter, Freeman, Pepi, and Reyna all scored or started. The heavily rotated side took Türkiye to a 90+8 winner and we still won the group. And Malik Tillman, "useful piece," scored in both knockout games. The depth did not just hold when Pulisic went down. The depth won us the group.

What we want to see

The signs USA is on.

1. Vertical entries through midfield. Without Musah on the roster, USA needs Adams + McKennie / Berhalter to do the line-breaking. If midfielders carry past the first line, things happen. If they don't, they don't.

2. Pulisic finishing chances. Not creating. Finishing. His actual goal-output is the leading indicator.

3. Adams covering the box. If he's clearing crosses and bumping attackers off the ball in his own 18, the rest of the back line stays compact.

4. Subs that change the game. Pochettino with a working bench is a contender. Pochettino with a one-eleven-deep group is a Round of 16 team.

Grade: two showed up, one showed up sideways, one keeps a little mystery. Sign 1, qualified yes: I cannot chart every line-breaking carry, so I will only say what the record supports, that the Australia performance came with 62 percent of the ball and McKennie, Adams, and Tillman all on the field together, and it looked like a team that knew what it was. Sign 2, no, and it did not matter the way I said it would: Pulisic's goal output read zero all summer, the "leading indicator" never lit, and we won the group anyway. Sign 3, the part I can verify is the part that counted: Adams' health held through the entire run, which was always the real ask underneath the sign, and the Belgium night broke behind the whole team, not one man. Sign 4, the loudest yes on the page, with the joke on me: we got the working bench, Trusty and Berhalter and Freeman and Pepi and Reyna all scoring or starting, and we were still a Round of 16 team. The bench was never the ceiling. The ceiling lived one section up, at the backline-versus-elite-attacks line.

The final grade

Pochettino's tournament, in one paragraph.

He won the group with a rotation discipline this page did not predict and could not have: a 3-5-2 nobody scouted, a wholesale-rotated eleven that nearly took the third match too, and trust in 26 players instead of a first-choice eleven. He won a knockout game with ten men for the last half hour, the program's first knockout win since 2002 and second ever. And he met the ceiling exactly where the honest assessment above said it lived, against an elite attack in transition, on a night when the scoreline told the truth. That is the best World Cup this program has had on home soil since 1994. It hurt to lose, and it was still the run. Both are true, and I am grateful I wrote the hard parts down in May so I could face them honestly in August.
