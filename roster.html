The 26 · Named May 26 · Home July 6

The Roster.

Pochettino announced his final 26 in New York on May 26. Every card from that day is still here, and each one now carries what the tournament actually was for that player. Notable omissions: Yunus Musah, Diego Luna, Cameron Carter-Vickers, Johnny Cardoso, Tanner Tessmann, Aidan Morris, Josh Sargent. Surprise inclusions: Gio Reyna, Alex Freeman, Alejandro Zendejas, Sebastian Berhalter. Three of those four surprises scored. Trust the coach.

3 goalkeepers

Between the posts.

Matt Freese
GK · New York City FC · Likely #1

Freese has held the spot through the Gold Cup and the spring friendlies. Sharp shot-stopping, calm distribution. The Belgium and Portugal friendlies exposed the defense in front of him, not him.

The tournament: Started the biggest matches over Turner, including the Australia shutout and the ten-man knockout win. Then the Belgium night, and the 57th minute clearance he misjudged that became Vanaken's goal. One bad read on the worst night, after a summer of being the answer. Both things are true, and only one of them should follow him.

Matt Turner
GK · New England Revolution

The veteran. 2022 World Cup starter. Returned to New England Revolution after his Premier League stint. Now serves as senior locker-room voice and No. 2 / No. 3 depth.

The tournament: Started the rotated Türkiye night at SoFi with the knockouts already clinched, and was the senior voice the rest of the way.

Chris Brady
GK · Chicago Fire

The young goalkeeper. MLS minutes with Chicago. Strong reach. The future of the position.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

10 defenders

The back line.

Chris Richards
CB · Crystal Palace · Starter if fit

The starting right centerback when healthy. Aerial dominance, ball-playing comfort, FA Cup form late in the season. An ankle injury on May 17 made him a tournament doubt; watch the friendlies. The most upside-y young CB in the pool.

The tournament: Fit when it counted. Started the Australia shutout in the back three of the 3-5-2, part of the group that won Group D and a knockout game with ten men.

Tim Ream
CB · Charlotte FC · Captain · 38

The grown-up in the room. 38 years old, wears the armband. Multiple World Cups. Left centerback. Reads the game three moves ahead.

The tournament: Captain, and every bit of the armband at 38. Anchored the back three in the Seattle shutout and led this group through a group win, a ten-man knockout win, and the hardest night, with the same steadiness in all three.

Antonee Robinson
LB · Fulham · Starter

"Jedi." One of the fastest fullbacks in the world. Engine on the left flank. Late runs and crosses for days.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Sergiño Dest
RB · PSV · Returning from injury

Back on the roster after the long ACL recovery. If healthy and starting, USA's most attacking fullback. If not, Scally holds the spot.

The tournament: Healthy and starting. Ran a wing-back lane in the Seattle 3-5-2, back on the biggest stage after the long ACL road.

Joe Scally
RB · Borussia Mönchengladbach

Two-footed, technically clean. Defensively first, attacking when invited. Likely starts at RB if Dest isn't full-go.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Miles Robinson
CB · FC Cincinnati

Backup CB. Aerial power, pace recovery. If injuries hit, the next man in.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Mark McKenzie
CB · Toulouse

Ligue 1 minutes. Comfortable left-footed CB. Useful for the 3-4-3 alternate when Pochettino goes three at the back.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Auston Trusty
CB · Celtic

Scottish Premiership minutes with Celtic. Big body, comfortable on the ball. Late-tournament rotation piece.

The tournament: A 3rd minute header off a corner against Türkiye. Three minutes in and SoFi was already shaking, and I was in the building for it. "Rotation piece" turned goalscorer.

7 midfielders

The midfield.

Tyler Adams
DM · AFC Bournemouth · Captain class · Anchor

The defensive spine. The number 6. If he's healthy and starting, the midfield holds. If not, the team has to rebalance.

The tournament: Started the Australia shutout in the middle of the 3-5-2. This page said if he starts, the midfield holds. It held.

Weston McKennie
CM · Juventus · Box-to-box

Most physical midfielder in the squad. Aerial threat, late-arriving runs into the box. Versatile, can play higher or deeper depending on the matchup.

The tournament: Started the Australia win in the engine room of the 3-5-2, part of the 26 that won Group D and a knockout game with ten men.

Sebastian Berhalter
CM · Vancouver Whitecaps · Surprise inclusion

MLS form earned him the call. Son of former USMNT head coach Gregg Berhalter. Likely cover for Adams.

The tournament: Started the Türkiye night and scored the 49th minute goal at SoFi. The surprise inclusion who put his own name on a World Cup scoresheet.

Cristian Roldan
CM · Seattle Sounders

Veteran MLS midfielder. Two-way energy, tactical reliability. Bench piece, can step in across midfield roles.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Malik Tillman
AM · Bayer Leverkusen

Bayern academy, PSV breakthrough, Leverkusen this year. Big late-tournament asset if Pochettino needs an extra attacker.

The tournament: The breakout. Started the Australia win in the 3-5-2, then two free-kick goals, one in each knockout round, a US World Cup record. The 82nd minute against Bosnia with ten men on the field, and the 31st minute in Seattle that made it level for 61 seconds of belief.

Alejandro Zendejas

Liga MX standout at Club América. Eligibility switched from Mexico to USA. Surprise inclusion in the 26. Creative spark off the bench.

6 forwards

The attack.

Christian Pulisic
LW / AM · AC Milan · The talisman

The engine, the face. Comes into the tournament in the worst form of his professional career. A glute issue shut down his Milan season early as a precaution. He needs the opener to go right.

Set pieces, free kicks, corners all his. Penalties likely shared with Balogun.

The tournament: Zero goals, and still the axis. He made the opener happen almost by himself, forcing the own goal and setting up Balogun's first, then left at halftime with calf tightness. Missed Australia. Came back, and in Seattle his foot met Tielemans' boot in the 52nd minute and the night went with him. The captain of hearts, if not the armband.

Folarin Balogun
ST · AS Monaco · #9 Starter

The starting striker. Penalty taker. Needs sharper movement against deep blocks like Paraguay's. Ligue 1 minutes give him the level expected at a World Cup.

The tournament: Three goals, the most of any American. The brace in the opener made him the first American with a multi-goal World Cup game since 1930. Then the goal against Bosnia and, sixteen minutes later, the 61st minute red card in the same knockout game. FIFA suspended the ban, Belgium's protest was thrown out hours before kickoff, and he got his Round of 16. A whole tournament of storylines in one striker.

Tim Weah
RW · Marseille

Pace on the right. Defensive work rate. Tournament version of him will be on the wing or as a wing-back in the 3-4-3 alternate.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

Gio Reyna
AM / W · Borussia Mönchengladbach · SURPRISE INCLUSION

Back in after a long absence from the squad. Pochettino's gamble. If healthy and right, an X-factor between the lines. If not, an expensive bench piece.

The tournament: The 90+8 toe-flick against Paraguay, his first World Cup goal, and the only word for it is redemption. Started the Türkiye night too. The gamble paid.

Ricardo Pepi
ST · PSV Eindhoven

Eredivisie minutes at PSV. Plan B striker. Different shape than Balogun: more box-presence, less linking.

The tournament: Started the Australia win alongside Balogun when Pulisic's calf ruled him out. Plan B, called on in Seattle, and the team won 2-0.

Haji Wright
ST · Coventry City

The big-body alternative. Air-game asset. Late-game battering ram.

The tournament: Part of the 26 that won Group D and took the program to its first knockout win since 2002.

The cuts

Notable omissions from the 26.

Yunus Musah (AC Milan), midfielder. The most surprising omission. Previously assumed lock-in, dropped from the final 26.

Diego Luna (Real Salt Lake), attacking midfielder. Massive cut after a strong Gold Cup. Reyna's inclusion came at his expense.

Cameron Carter-Vickers (Celtic), centerback. Recovering from a torn Achilles in October 2025. Not full-go for the tournament.

Johnny Cardoso (Real Betis), defensive midfielder. Pochettino went for ball-progressors over runners.

Tanner Tessmann (Olympique Lyonnais), midfielder. Lyon minutes but didn't fit the final shape.

Aidan Morris (Middlesbrough), midfielder. Out of the squad.

Josh Sargent (Norwich City), striker. Championship form but the four-forward shape went elsewhere.
