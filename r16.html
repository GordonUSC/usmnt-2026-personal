The actual path, walked.

This page existed to predict our Round of 16 opponent, and it said Belgium. Belgium came. In front of 66,925 in Seattle on July 6, they beat us 4-1 and our World Cup ended. The prophecy page, fulfilled, just not the way I wanted. The match report is up top now; the prediction is kept below, exactly as written, because we called the path right and I want the receipt.

The match · Monday July 6 · Lumen Field · attendance 66,925

Belgium. The whistle in Seattle.

It went wrong early. Charles De Ketelaere finished a Castagne cross in the 8th or 9th minute, depending on whose clock you trust, and we were chasing before the nerves had settled.

Then came the 61 seconds I will keep. In the 31st minute Malik Tillman stood over a free kick and hit it, the wall deflected it in, and it was 1-1. His second free-kick goal in as many knockout games. For 61 seconds it was level and everything felt possible. Then Belgium took the restart, worked it straight up the field, and De Ketelaere scored again in the 33rd. Level for a minute and one second. The cruelest stat of my summer.

The second half took the rest. In the 52nd Christian Pulisic went down when his foot struck Tielemans' boot; he tried to carry on and was off around the 59th. In between, in the 57th, Matt Freese missed his clearance and Hans Vanaken put Belgium up 3-1. Lukaku added the fourth deep in stoppage time, 90+3'. Full time: Belgium 4, USA 1, and Belgium's unbeaten run stretched to 18 games. We were the 18th.

The vindication, such as it is

We lost to the team that pushed the champions hardest.

This page said Spain's lane fed our quarterfinal. It did. The QF seat we were playing for went to Belgium instead, and Belgium met Spain and lost only 2-1. Spain then won the whole tournament, conceding exactly one goal all summer. Sit with that math: the only goal anyone scored on the eventual champions was scored by the team that knocked us out.

So here is the honest read. We did not stumble into some fluke ending. We lost to the side that pushed the champions harder than anyone else in the tournament. And the 5-2 friendly in March, the one this page talked itself into treating as a B-tier asterisk, turned out to be a truer omen than we hoped. Belgium was better than us, in March and in July. Both things get to be true: the scouting was right about who would be standing in Seattle, and wrong about how close we could make it.

Where I was when it ended

Stoughton, Wisconsin. Soccer fever.

I did not watch the end of our World Cup in a stadium. I watched it in a small town in Wisconsin, and I would not trade it. My own words that day: "I'm here in the Norwegian town of Stoughton, WISCONSIN! where soccer fever has struck in the past 24 hours." That morning Matt Kramer texted "MATCHDAY!!!" and I texted back "MATCHDAY", because that is the whole liturgy.

Then the match happened. Somewhere in there I texted "The goal was the most horrible defense", because loving a team means being honest about it. And after the whistle a friend wrote, "And that's a rap for our World Cup." I sat with that for a second. Then I answered with the truest sentence I typed all summer: "Maybe the World Cup was the friends we made along the way." That sentence earned its own page. Go read it.

The honest ledger: it hurt. It still hurts a little, and I will not pretend the fourth goal did not land like a door closing. And this run was still the program's best home-soil World Cup since 1994. Group won. A ten-man knockout win. Sixty-one seconds level with the team that nearly beat the champions. And I got to watch the ending in a town flying Norwegian flags, one that caught soccer fever in a single day. Both things are true. That is what loving a team is.

A correction worth being clear about

Where we are vs where the giants are.

USA, as Group D winner, sits in Match 81 of the Round of 32. The R16 pairs winners of Match 81 vs Match 82. That R16 match is Match 94, on Monday July 6 at Lumen Field in Seattle.

Match 82 is: Group G winner vs the best 3rd-place team from Group A, E, H, I, or J.

So our R16 opponent will be one of two things:

  • The Group G winner, almost certainly Belgium (the heavy favorite to win that group).
  • A 3rd-place team from Group A, E, H, I, or J, if they upset Belgium in R32.

That's it for the R16. None of the giants can show up here. But they are not all parked in the other half of the bracket: Spain's path feeds our Quarterfinal and Brazil's feeds our Semifinal. Only England and Portugal sit in the true other half, where the earliest meeting is the Final. Full map below.

The most likely R16 opponent

Belgium.

Belgium (Red Devils)
Group G winner · Coach Rudi Garcia · 3-4-3

Star: Kevin De Bruyne.

The shape

The Golden Generation's last dance. KDB the heartbeat. Lukaku up top. Doku and Trossard out wide. Aging but lethal in transition.

Strengths

Knockout-football pedigree. KDB's ability to flip a game with one pass. Lukaku's hold-up play.

Weaknesses

Pace at centerback. Set-piece defense. The dressing-room age dynamic.

How USA could compete

Press KDB high. He cannot create against pressure the way he used to. Force Belgium to defend transitions at pace with their veteran back line. Belgium beat USA 5-2 in March in a friendly with a B-tier USA midfield. With a full-strength group and tournament stakes, this is much closer than that scoreline.

The 50/50 question

Does Belgium even win Group G? They are clear favorites, but Egypt has Salah, Iran has Mehdi Taremi, and any tournament can surprise. If Belgium finishes 2nd or 3rd in their group, we're playing someone else here.

The 3rd-place upset scenario

If a 3rd-place team beats Belgium in R32.

The other path: a 3rd-place team from Group A, E, H, I, or J beats Belgium in Match 82, then plays USA in R16. The candidates from those groups, ordered by how plausible they are:

Senegal
If 3rd in Group I (France, Senegal, Norway, Iraq)

Africa's most consistent side. Mané, Koulibaly. If France wins Group I and Norway finishes 2nd, Senegal could be the 3rd-place team here. A real test.

Uruguay
If 3rd in Group H (Spain, Cape Verde, Saudi Arabia, Uruguay)

Bielsa's vicious counter-attacking side. Valverde + Núñez. If Spain wins the group and Saudi or Cape Verde takes second, Uruguay finishing 3rd is plausible. The most dangerous possible 3rd-place team in this entire branch.

Austria
If 3rd in Group J (Argentina, Algeria, Austria, Jordan)

Rangnick's high-press machine. Argentina is the lock-in for 1st; Austria most likely 2nd, but could slip to 3rd if Algeria has a moment.

Ivory Coast
If 3rd in Group E (Germany, Curaçao, Ivory Coast, Ecuador)

AFCON 2023 champions. Could finish 3rd behind Germany and Ecuador. Same team we profiled in R32 as a possibility.

South Korea
If 3rd in Group A (Mexico, South Africa, South Korea, Czechia)

Son's likely last World Cup. If Mexico and Czechia finish ahead, Son's Korea could be a 3rd-place team here. Lethal in transition.

Where the giants actually live

Brazil, Spain, Portugal, England.

None of these four can be the R16 opponent. But their paths converge with ours at different rounds, and two of them arrive earlier than the casual read suggests.

Quarterfinal: Spain's lane

USA's QF is Match 98, Friday July 10 at SoFi Stadium: Winner of Match 94 (USA's R16) vs Winner of Match 93. Match 93 draws from the R32 match that Spain's expected path feeds (Match 84 → Match 93 → Match 98). If Spain wins Group H and keeps winning, the Quarterfinal is USA v Spain, at SoFi. That's the earliest possible giant.

Semifinal: Brazil's lane

The Semifinal pairs our QF winner with the winner of Match 97, which Brazil's expected path feeds (Match 89 → Match 97). Survive Spain and the reward is plausibly Brazil.

Final: England and Portugal's half

Only England and Portugal live in the true other half of the bracket. The earliest USA can meet either is the Final, July 19 at MetLife.

So when is the giant matchup?

For the foreseeable USA path: Spain in the QF, Brazil in the SF, England or Portugal only in the Final. The gauntlet is real, and it starts at SoFi.

How it actually played out · coda added August 1

The Spain lane was exactly right: the winner of our R16 went on to face Spain in the quarterfinal, and Belgium lost it 2-1. The Brazil semifinal hypothetical never came due, because Norway beat Brazil 2-0 in the Round of 16 and the lane rerouted. England did live in the other half, and took third place in that ridiculous 6-4 bronze game. Portugal is the one the map missed: the bracket dropped them into Spain's lane after all, and Spain beat them 1-0 in the Round of 16, one round before Belgium. So the gauntlet was real. We just never got to walk it.
