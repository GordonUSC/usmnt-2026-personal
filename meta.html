The meta chapter · How a fan and his AI sidekick spent a World Cup

My World Cup had
a co-pilot.

While the tournament ran, something else was running too. Darby and I spent the summer building: sites for my people, guides for strangers, games for watch parties, and this archive itself, updated loop by loop, sometimes from the parking lot of the stadium. 2026 was the first AI World Cup. All over the internet, fans built ticket trackers and prediction models and wallcharts with AI at their side. This page is our corner of that story, and the rules we built it by.

Everything on this site is real. This page is about the making, which was also real, and also joyful.
G

The sentence that explains the whole summer

The instruction I gave Darby in June.

On June 16, weighing a schedule conflict against a World Cup invitation, I did not write Darby a pros-and-cons list. I wrote this:

"explain to him the meaning of attending the world cup to me, based on what you know from having built my websites"

That is the meta of this whole thing. The sites were never just sites. They were where the meaning lived, organized well enough that even an AI could read it back to someone on my behalf. Build your joy down in a form you can point to. That is the lesson of my summer.

The output · June and July 2026

What we built while the Cup ran.

A small studio of two, one of us made of matter. Every build below shipped during or around the tournament, for a real person, with a real occasion.

This site, in loops
May 27 onward · for my friends and family

Born as a friends-and-family guide, fact-checked hard after I caught it hallucinating rosters in May, then updated in numbered loops as the tournament moved: predict-the-score before Türkiye, the knockout-path explainer, the matchday countdowns. After the final whistle it graduated into this memory archive. The full prompt series that drove the August rebuild is public: PROMPTS.md.

Opening Weekend LA
June 11 to 14 · for Quinn

A whole site for one visit: Quinn's opener trip, with a penalty-kick game, match bingo with badges, a watch-and-sip guide for every match of the weekend, and an MLS-players tracker, because Quinn and I are American Outlaws and the details matter. The bookends of our group stage both had Quinn in them, and the site was the itinerary.

Laylah's World Cup
June 23 · Houston · live at laylah-wc

Laylah flew to Houston for Portugal v Uzbekistan, so we built her a trip site: her stadium, her stays, her matchday. Seven-star service for chosen family. It is still live, because memories deserve permalinks.

The Supporters' Path
public edition · live at usmnt-2026-public

The shareable public cousin of this site: roster, tactics, lore, American Outlaws culture, no personal receipts. Built so I could hand strangers something without handing them my life.

The fan guide
built the day before the Türkiye match · for anyone

"Your matchday, handled." Metro directions, clear-bag rules, Soccer 101, chants, a tap-to-cheer meter. Built in a day, used that same week, now archived as a time capsule with a photo atlas inside. It worked.

The house rules the making taught us

How to build a memory you can trust.

Receipts or it stays out
Presence is never asserted without a ticket, a text, or a photo. Where we lack one, the site says so out loud.
Real is the aesthetic
Full color for memory photos. Duotone for stock. Labels for art. The difference is the design.
Grade your own predictions
We kept every scouting report we wrote and graded it after. Being wrong in public is a feature.
Build for one person
Every site had a name on it: Quinn's weekend, Laylah's Houston, Pete's chapter, Foster's birthday. Scale came from love, not from reach.
Some things stay human
Eric's menu is not documented. The quietest moment sits behind a press-and-hold. Not everything is content.
The site is never done
Text me a memory and the record improves. A living archive is the point of having a co-pilot.
